FIU · Graduate Assistant Onboarding

FIU International PhD Students Checklist

Congratulations! You got into the PhD Public Health Program (HPDP) — now what?

1

Accept Your Admission and Wait for Your Funding Letter

Here's the order these emails actually show up in:

  1. First, you get an email from your department confirming your admission.
  2. Next, you get an email from the School of Public Health (HPDP). That's your green light to activate MyFIU.
  3. Your funding (GA) letter usually takes a few more days to land. If it's dragging, follow up with your program director — they're the one who's been emailing you this whole time.

Once you've got both your admission letter and your funding letter, you're ready for Step 2 and your I-20.

2

Get Your I-20 (Proof You're Approved to Study Here)

What it is: Your I-20 is basically FIU's stamp of approval. It proves you're a real, full-time international student who can afford to be here. No I-20 means no visa and no getting into the U.S. as a student.

Once you've got your admission letter and your funding letter (Step 1), you're ready to start this. FIU just needs to confirm you can cover your costs before they'll issue your I-20.

3

Pay the SEVIS Fee and Get Your Visa

What it is: The SEVIS I-901 fee is a one-time government fee that activates your student record in the U.S. immigration system (SEVIS). Your F-1 visa is the stamp in your passport that lets you travel to the U.S. as a student.

Why it matters: You can't book your visa appointment until the SEVIS fee is paid, and you can't travel without the visa. As soon as your I-20 arrives, move on this — visa appointment slots fill up, and that timeline is outside FIU's control.

4

Keep an Eye on MyFIU

What it is: MyFIU is your personal FIU account portal. A "hold" is something blocking you from registering for classes until you fix it, like a missing document.

A hold is not something to discover the week classes start.

5

Clear Your Immunization and Insurance Holds

Why it matters: Either an immunization hold or an insurance hold can stop you from registering for classes, even if you're a fully funded GA (Graduate Assistant). Do this at least 4 weeks before registration.

6

Register for Classes and Find Housing

F-1 grad students need 9 credits per Fall/Spring semester: at least 6 in person, no more than 3 online counting toward that 9. Start looking for housing at the same time — don't wait until you land.

7

Start GA/HR Onboarding Before You Arrive

What it is: A GA (Graduate Assistant) position is paid work for the university, done alongside your PhD program. Getting this set up early means your first paycheck isn't delayed once you arrive.

Do this before you leave for the U.S., not after.

8

Complete Pre-Arrival Requirements

FIU requires this before check-in, and requires these documents at entry.

9

Travel to the U.S.

Why it matters: Entering as an F-1 student, not a tourist, keeps your legal student status intact from day one. Entering the wrong way can cause serious problems later. You can enter as early as 30 days before your I-20 program start date — arrive at the start of that window, since payroll processing takes time once you land.

10

Right After You Land

What these are: Your I-94 is your official U.S. arrival record. SEVIS is the government system that tracks international students; it must show ACTIVE for you to legally study and work here.

This step unlocks everything after it. Your SEVIS record only goes ACTIVE once ISSS (International Student & Scholar Services) check-in is complete.

11

Get Employment Certification and Apply for Your SSN

What it is: Your SSN (Social Security Number) is a 9-digit U.S. government ID number. You need it to get paid, to be added to payroll, and for everyday things like opening a bank account.

Why it matters: No SSN, no paycheck. This is one of the most important steps on this whole list.

12

Finish GA Sign-On and I-9

What it is: The I-9 is a federal form that proves you're allowed to work in the U.S. Every employee fills one out, not just international students.

You do not have to wait for the physical SSN card to start this.

13

Wait for Clearance, Then Payroll

Don't start GA work early, even informally.

Be Patient, But Persistent

You'll interact with multiple FIU offices, and one office may be waiting on another before it can finish your request. Keep copies of every document. Check your FIU email and MyFIU regularly. Follow up politely. Don't assume silence means the process is complete.

♥

One More Thing: Take Care of Your Mental Health

Moving to a new country, starting a PhD, and juggling visa paperwork all at once is a lot. FIU has free, confidential counseling for students.
